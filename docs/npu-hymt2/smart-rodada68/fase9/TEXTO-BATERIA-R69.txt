The city council approved on Tuesday a comprehensive plan to modernize the
municipal water network over the next five years. According to the proposal,
aging pipes in the central district will be replaced first, since recent
inspections found leaks responsible for nearly a third of the city's daily
water loss. Engineers estimate that the project will reduce consumption by
about eighteen percent once the first phase is complete.

Local businesses welcomed the announcement, although some owners asked for
clearer schedules to avoid disruptions during the busiest months of the year.
The council promised weekly updates and a public dashboard showing the
progress of each street. Environmental groups, in turn, requested that the
plan include stricter controls on industrial discharge, arguing that pipe
replacement alone will not protect the rivers that cross the region.

The mayor said the investment will be funded through a combination of
municipal savings and a long-term loan, and insisted that household bills
will not increase before the second phase. A public hearing is scheduled for
next month, when residents will be able to present suggestions and formally
question the technical team responsible for the project.
